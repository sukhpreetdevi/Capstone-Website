Research and healthy digital habits

Make space for the whole picture.

Recreational screen time is not one behaviour and it is not automatically harmful. Context, timing, purpose, and personal needs all matter.

Three types of technology use

Ask what the screen is doing for you.

Academic: lectures, readings, research, assignments, and collaboration.

Essential: banking, communication, health care, navigation, and administration.

Recreational: streaming, gaming, scrolling, browsing, and digital leisure.

Research stance

Associations are signals, not diagnoses.

Research can show that two patterns occur together. It cannot automatically prove that one caused the other.

Five practical invitations

Small experiments beat all-or-nothing rules.

01

Protect a wind-down window

Try a calmer transition before bed.

02

Make the default visible

Review notifications and home-screen placement.

03

Pair scrolling with a pause

Before opening a feed, ask what you need from it.

04

Use movement as a reset

Stand, stretch, or walk during long sessions.

05

Reflect without judgement

Record time, purpose, mood, and what you chose next.

Questions worth asking

Keep the conversation open.

What might a sleep-focused reflection include?

Consider timing, stimulation, content, and whether the device helps you wind down or keeps you engaged.

How can I think about academic functioning?

Notice whether recreational use interrupts a task, helps you recover, or is being used to manage stress.

What does digital wellbeing mean?

Digital wellbeing means aligning technology use with your values, relationships, responsibilities, and rest.
