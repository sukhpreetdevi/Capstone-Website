Post 1 · Awareness

Are You Scrolling Without Noticing?

#HealthyDigitalHabits #MindfulScrolling #DigitalBalance #ScreenTimeAwareness

Do you ever open your phone to check something for a few minutes and end up scrolling longer than you planned? Recreational screen time is a part of everyday student life, but noticing our habits is an important first step toward better digital balance.” Today, take a moment to think about when, why and how long you scroll.

Post 2 · Information Carousel

Screen Time, Sleep, and Studying

#HealthyStudyHabits #StudentWellbeing #DigitalBalance #ScreenTimeAwareness

There could be a link between our smartphone habits and different parts of student life. Research has identified the association of problematic smartphone use with sleep difficulties among university students (Yin et al., 2025). Another literature review found a small negative relationship between problematic smartphone use and academic achievement (Paterna et al., 2024). This is not to say that all screen time is bad, or that smartphone use leads directly to these outcomes. The important message is to be aware of how, when and why we use technology

Post 3 · Impact and Action Carousel

Small Steps Towards Better Digital Balance.

#HealthyDigitalHabits #StudentWellbeing #DigitalBalance #MindfulTechnology

Small changes can set healthy digital habits in motion. Try turning off notifications you don't need while studying, trying a screen-free wind-down routine before bed, or picking one habit to work on this week. You don’t have to stop using technology. The aim is to be more intentional about your recreational screen time. What’s one digital habit you’d like to improve?

Instagram Reel

Just 5 More Minutes?

Description explaining purpose, design choices, and connection to research.

Purpose

The purpose of my Instagram campaign is to encourage university students to develop healthier recreational screen-time habits through awareness, research information, and practical suggestions. I created three Instagram posts and one short video using Canva. The first post encourages students to notice their scrolling habits, the second explains research findings about sleep and studying, and the third suggests small changes. The video reinforces the campaign message.

Design Choice

The colours I used for my design selections are the beige background (#F1E7D7) for Post 1, the brightly coloured red gradient for Post 2, and grey azure (#91B5C9) for Post 3. Added illustrations, photographs, simple headings and messages so that I could present information visually

Connection to Research

The campaign directly relates to my capstone research question because of studies by Yin et al. (2025) and Paterna et al. (2024) that have shown correlation between psychosocial problems with use of smartphones, sleep problems, and decreased academic performance. My campaign encourages balanced technology use and also translates these research findings into simple, practical messages that encourage students to reflect on their screentime habits and make healthier digital choices
